The question "Valkey or Upstash or Neo4j" is the wrong one. A shared store solves one problem: several machines needing the same state. Council 1 decided the live site stays on ONE machine, and the brief says process-local state is "CORRECT on one machine." So the live product has no requirement for Valkey. The only thing that wants multiple machines is the 1,000-user staging gate, and that need is itself unproven: S2 may show one performance-class machine passes.

The plan is solving a hypothetical. S1 and S3 add a recurring cost (about $8.65 per month for Valkey) and a new single point of failure with downtime on every deploy. Under fail-closed, a Valkey outage would pause paid answers on a live site that today has no such dependency. They also make staging a different architecture from live, so the passing gate would say less about the real site. The "Neo4j cannot carry it" premise is an assumption, and spike S7 has not run.

What we are actually solving: (a) make one machine hold about 40 live streams, which is the async rewrite and is not in question; (b) find out whether Neo4j service state at roughly 12 reads/s and 9 writes/s is a bottleneck, which is an unmeasured fact; (c) keep the interface swappable, which is cheap.

Answers: (1) S2. Evidence that flips it: S2 shows one machine cannot pass, S7 shows Neo4j saturating below 20 asks/s, or the owner wants a multi-machine live fleet. (2) If adopted later: self-hosted Valkey 9.1.2 or later, valkey-py through `limits`, AOF on with a volume, and fail closed for paid asks only. (3) Keep the $10/day cap, the three-level kill switch and the HMAC pepper; none of them depends on Valkey. (4) Order: async rewrite, then the protocol with in-process and neo4j backends, then S2, then S7. (5) Biggest risk: Neo4j service-state latency, caught early by running S7 at 5, 10 and 20 asks/s before anything else.

Recommendation: choose S2, and treat Valkey as a measured response to S2 and S7 results rather than a precondition.
