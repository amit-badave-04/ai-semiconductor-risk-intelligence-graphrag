# Council 4: One address can pause the paid day (H2/M3): abandoned-ask charging and a per-address spend share

Run 2026-10-07 (workflow wf_3680c395-34f): five Sonnet advisors, five Sonnet peer reviewers (anonymized), Opus chairman.

## Framed question

CONTEXT. Semigraph is a public demo site (one Fly.io machine) that answers questions about semiconductor companies from SEC filings, using LLMs; the owner pays the LLM bill personally. Owner-approved paid-ask policy: at most 150 paid ("live") asks per UTC day in total; a $10/day spend cap that is ESTIMATE-BASED (each ask reserves its upper-bound cost estimate at admission; when it completes it is settled at the provider-reported cost); at most 20 paid asks per visitor address per day (IPv4 address or IPv6 /64, HMAC-hashed); a paid window of 5 asks per 10 minutes per address; a Cloudflare Turnstile bot check on every paid ask; at most 2 asks in flight at once. Cached example answers are free and unaffected by any of this. When the daily count or the $10 cap is reached, every visitor's live questions are refused until UTC midnight ("paused"). When the owner approved the 20-per-address cap, the stated reasoning was that pausing the whole day should need at least 8 different addresses. Buyer demos may come from one office network (one address) and ask up to 20 live questions in a day; a preview for named buyers is planned.

MEASURED COSTS. A typical real answer costs $0.001-0.002 (cheap draft model gpt-6-luna at $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens). Some answers escalate to Claude Sonnet 5 ($2/$10 per million) after a failed citation check (typically a few cents). Upper-bound estimates per ask type (worst-case prompt with 4 companies, both Sonnet attempts, 2,400 output tokens): hybrid $0.58, workspace $0.60, vector $0.22, agent $0.83. At the worst-case prompt the cheap draft call alone is bounded at about $0.014 and one Sonnet escalation attempt at about $0.28; at a typical prompt (about 10k tokens) a Sonnet attempt is bounded at about $0.045.

THE FLAW (found independently by two reviewers and proven in tests). When a visitor disconnects mid-answer, the server cannot know what the provider billed, so the "abandoned" ask is charged its FULL estimate. One address can reserve and immediately disconnect: 17 hybrid or 12 agent asks (under its 20/day cap; about 35 minutes because of the paid window; one Turnstile pass each) push recorded spend to about $9.86 and pause live questions for everyone for the rest of the day, while real provider spend is near $0. Real spend never exceeds $10 (the money cap works), but the availability assumption ("needs at least 8 addresses") fails. An exhausted day cannot be reset by the admin (the production config refuses raised caps; only a kill switch exists).

A MECHANISM IS BEING BUILT ANYWAY (to fix a separate accounting bug where a retried or failed attempt's cost went unreported): every paid model call records, at the moment it starts, an upper bound computed from its ACTUAL prompt (prompt characters / 2.5 characters per token, an assumption 17% below the lowest measured ratio, plus its max output tokens at the model's price, times its retry attempts), and records its reported usage when it completes. So an abandoned ask COULD be charged "reported cost of completed calls + bound of calls started but not completed" (0 if no paid call started) instead of the full estimate. The in-flight reservation still holds the full estimate while the ask runs, so the $10 ceiling on real spend holds either way. An attacker can see the "escalated" event in the answer stream and disconnect right after the expensive Sonnet call starts, and questions that fail the citation check escalate.

OPTIONS BEING WEIGHED (not exhaustive):
(A) Charge abandoned asks the started-calls amount (the mechanism above) instead of the full estimate. Nothing else.
(B) A per-address daily spend share (e.g. $10/8 = $1.25), checked at admission against that address's already-settled spend plus this ask's estimate (a typical visitor, whose settled answers cost about $0.002 each, is never blocked by it; an address whose asks are charged heavily is).
(C) Both A and B.
(D) Keep full-estimate charging and accept the risk (Turnstile per ask, paid window, admin can watch); document it.
(E) Something else (e.g. an admin "forgive abandoned charges" action; charging abandoned asks to the address but not to the global cap; lowering estimates).
CONSTRAINTS: never under-count real provider spend against the $10 cap (no one may make the site spend more than $10/day); keep a buyer demo from one address working (20 live asks/day); no new paid services; one machine, small codebase, every change is test-first and reviewed; production refuses raised caps. A wrong choice either lets one person deny the live demo for a day (bad during a buyer preview) or weakens the money cap.

QUESTION: Which option should be built now, with what parameters, and why?
