Status: **approved to build** by the owner on 2026-09-26 ("no temporary fixes, stop live, fix the things the correct way, then push/deploy. We have time. No shortcuts."; order: M1b, then a thin agent, then thin upload). The live site is stopped until M1b is verified.
Source: independent architect design (Fable) on the basis of `REVIEW_2026-09-26.md`; the architect could not browse the web, so the method references in section B must be confirmed against primary sources before they are cited. Numbers marked (lake) were computed by the architect from `data/`; the audit's counts differ slightly (audit: 39 annual risk sections, 15 partial; lake: 45 / 17 because the two 20-F filers use Item 3.D / section `I.3`).

## 0. Goal: three things true before anything else is built
1. A "dropped risk" is a risk whose text is genuinely absent from the newer annual filing, **verified against that filing's full text**.
2. Numbers in answers are labelled by fiscal year and every number is traceable to an XBRL fact id or a cited chunk.
3. The evaluation instrument can detect exactly the failures the audit found: **it must grade the currently served T1 and T3 example answers `incorrect`.**

Code facts that constrain the design: chunk ids are positional and append-only (`CITE_RE` in `retrieval/answerer.py`, `CHUNK_ID_RE` in `serve/routes.py`); chunks carry `char_start`/`char_end` into `data/interim/section_texts`, so new units can be mapped to chunk ids without touching chunk ids; `Metric.metric_id = {cik}:{metric}:{period_end}` already exists and is unique (`graph/loaders.py`, `schema.cypher`) and is a ready-made citable XBRL id; lineage closure is `graph/temporal.py` (greedy 0.75 centroid clustering of LLM summaries); `scripts/verify_graph.py` has no temporal or numeric invariant; `snapshot.py` covers extraction/chunk/section-text hashes, so these changes force a new snapshot and regenerated examples. Extraction facts (lake): every LATEST annual risk section is 100% extracted for all 13 filers; 473 Item 1A/3.D chunks (~284k tokens) have no extraction record; the chunker's `sub_heading` is the per-risk headline only for NVDA, MU, AMZN, part of MSFT and QCOM, but raw HTML preserves bold per-risk headlines for AMD and META; Intel and the 20-F filers do not use bold headlines reliably.

## A. Ground truth first (source-text gold)
- **Unit of truth: the risk item** = one risk-factor headline plus its body paragraphs inside Item 1A (10-K) or Item 3.D (20-F), located by character span, with the chunk ids it overlaps. Where no headline is detectable (Intel, ASML, partly TSMC) the unit is a paragraph (`unit_kind=paragraph`). The "Risk Factors Summary" bullet block must be excluded or every headline appears twice.
- **Labels for an older item against the newer filing's FULL section text:** `unchanged` (content hash equal), `reworded` (labeller quotes the matching text verbatim), `merged` (absorbed elsewhere; quote required), `removed` (labeller lists the search terms tried). Symmetric per newer item: `carried` or `new`. **Every quote is verified programmatically by containment** in the newer section (reuse the normalisation in `extraction/gates.py::quote_in_chunk`); a label whose quote does not exist is rejected. This is the anti-fabrication gate the earlier calibration lacked.
- **Sampling:** 32 consecutive annual pairs. Label completely: NVDA FY25→FY26, NVDA FY24→FY25, AMD FY25→FY26, META FY25→FY26, MU FY24→FY25, TSM FY25→FY26 (development set: headline-rich, headline-poor, 20-F). For the other 26 pairs label every item the algorithm marks removed/new or places in the uncertainty band, plus 5 random unchanged/reworded per pair (held-out set for the final numbers). Expected 450–600 item labels.
- **Protocol:** three blind sub-agent labellers per pair (session tokens, not API dollars; each reads the older item list and the full newer section), majority decides, disagreements and rejected quotes go to an Opus adjudicator. Freeze only when pairwise agreement ≥ 0.90 and Krippendorff α ≥ 0.80 on the four classes; else fix the instructions and relabel the development pairs.
- **Owner human spot labels (30–40 items, about one hour):** every algorithm-predicted drop for NVDA FY25→FY26 (roughly 5–10), 10 uncertainty-band items, 10 random "present" items. Owner-vs-gold agreement must be ≥ 0.90 or the gold is not accepted. The gold is frozen with a sha256 in `artifacts/gold/risk_items_gold.json` **before any threshold tuning**.
- **Numeric gold:** deterministic from XBRL: for each filer and metric (revenue, net income, R&D, operating income) consecutive fiscal-year values and code-computed year-over-year. 24 questions (same-metric YoY, cross-metric same-year, two-year growth) with exact expected values, plus a 40-answer labelled set from saved runs where sub-agents mark each number `grounded` / `ungrounded`, to measure the numeric checker's false-positive/negative rate.
- **Temporal answer-quality gold:** derived from the item gold, never from the graph: per fully labelled pair, the verified lists of removed/new/reworded headlines with chunk ids; these become judge notes for T-questions.

## B. Algorithm (pure Python, unit-testable, cheapest first)
1. **Unit detection** (`parsing/risk_items.py`, new): recover headlines from the raw HTML (`data/raw/edgar/<T>/…html`; bold spans of 8–60 words inside the risk section, de-duplicated against the summary block), locate them in the section text, cut items at headline boundaries, map to chunk ids by `char_start`/`char_end` overlap. Fallback: paragraph units. Emit a per-filing coverage figure (share of section characters inside items). No LLM.
2. **Exact match:** normalised content hash (`hashing.py` spec) → `unchanged`.
3. **Headline match:** `rapidfuzz.fuzz.token_set_ratio(headline_a, headline_b) >= 90` → `reworded` unless bodies hash-equal.
4. **Body similarity, one-to-one:** cosine of Qwen3 embeddings of full item text (local, free) plus a lexical score (`difflib.SequenceMatcher.ratio` or 5-gram containment); assignment by `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` with a floor. Accept when embed ≥ 0.85 and lexical ≥ 0.45; reject when embed < 0.65; the band in between is adjudicated.
5. **Text-grounded absence check before any `removed`:** search the ENTIRE newer section sentence by sentence with `rapidfuzz.fuzz.partial_ratio >= 85` for the item's headline and its two longest sentences; a hit means `merged`/`moved`, not removed. **This is the check the current code never does.**
6. **Band adjudication:** a cheap LLM (GPT-6 Luna, three-way verdict, both texts): about 100–200 calls, $0.05–0.20. Record `decided_by` on every edge. Owner decision: use Luna, measured on the gold; escalate to Sonnet only if Luna's agreement on the gold is poor.
- **Comparison and decision rule:** run A = current clustering (baseline), B = steps 1–5, C = steps 1–6 on the **held-out** gold; report drop precision/recall, new-item precision/recall, reworded accuracy. Accept the first of C, B that reaches drop precision ≥ 0.90 and recall ≥ 0.80 on held-out pairs AND precision ≥ 0.95 on the NVDA flagship pair. If neither does, report per-filer numbers and restrict "dropped" narration to filers that pass (others get "text changed" only).
- **Methods to confirm from primary sources (about 15 minutes, before citing):** Ratcliff–Obershelp (difflib docs), rapidfuzz scorer semantics, Hungarian assignment (scipy docs), Vecalign (Thompson and Koehn, EMNLP 2019) as a monotonic alternative, and the finance literature on 10-K year-over-year change (Cohen, Malloy, Nguyen "Lazy Prices", J. Finance 2020; Brown, Tian, Tucker 2018).
- **Graph consequences:** new `RiskItem` nodes are the lineage unit; LLM-extracted `RiskFactor` nodes become annotations attached by chunk overlap (`OF_ITEM`) and are not used for lineage decisions. `DISCLOSES_RISK.status` keeps `Active`/`Deleted` (**decision: keep the values, change the semantics** so `Deleted` means text-verified removed; add `removed_in` and `verification='text'`). Years with no extraction still get items and lineages (headlines are verbatim, citable text), so unequal extraction no longer corrupts comparison.

## C. Coverage gate and re-extraction
- The gate becomes **unit coverage**: a consecutive pair enters lineage computation only if both sections have item coverage ≥ 90% of characters and were parsed from the effective filing per `versions.py` (AMD's 10-K/A restates only Item 7, so its risk items come from the original). Failing pairs are listed in the build output and `/api/stats`, never compared silently. Extraction coverage is reported per filing but does not gate lineages.

- **Tier 2 (not now; $2.9 likely / $4.4 worst by the repo's estimator, over $1 so needs the owner's approval):** all 473 chunks. It only buys LLM summaries on historical items, which nothing in M1b needs.

## D. Retrieval and prompt changes
- New shared module `retrieval/ids.py` (edited first, serially): chunk id grammar unchanged; add `xbrl:<cik>:<metric>:<period_end>` and `fr:<document_number>`; one `CITE_RE` shared by `answerer.py`, `verify.py`, `routes.py` (`CHUNK_ID_RE` becomes a dispatcher; `/api/evidence/{id}` resolves xbrl ids to the Metric node plus accession, and fr ids to the rule).
- `METRICS_QUERY`: per metric the last three fiscal periods (`CALL` subquery, not "newest 20 rows"); block grouped by "fiscal year ended <period_end>", each line carrying its xbrl id; year-over-year computed in code ("computed: +65.5% vs FY ended 2025-01-26"); always state the period end date.
- `TEMPORAL_QUERY` replaced by an item-based query returning removed / new / reworded items with headline, older/newer chunk ids and `decided_by`, ranked headline-first then by length and similarity to the question, capped 8/8/4 with totals stated; header "RISK FACTORS REMOVED / ADDED / REWORDED between <older> and <newer> (text-verified)".
- Rule edges block relabelled "EXTERNAL REGULATORY EVENTS (Federal Register rules linked by keyword; not the company's disclosure)", each dated with an `fr:` id; `answer.txt` gains "never describe an external event as something the company disclosed; cite it as [fr:…]"; AFFECTED_BY edges gain `source='federal_register'`, `link_method='keyword'`, `external=true`.
- `verify.py`: numeric grounding for **every** answer (each money value must match a context value; each percentage must match a computed YoY or appear in a cited chunk; tolerance 0.5%); reason `ungrounded_number`. Strong-routed answers get a `checks` object in `done` (`citations_retrieved`, `numbers_grounded`, `unmatched_numbers`) instead of silently passing.
- Evidence drawer returns form, fiscal period, section title, item headline; for xbrl ids the value and accession.

## K. Research corrections (verified from primary sources and measured on our lake, 2026-09-26; these SUPERSEDE the matching details above)
1. **difflib is not Ratcliff–Obershelp** (its docs say it "predates, and is a little fancier than" it): greedy longest-block recursion, `autojunk` on by default and asymmetric. Character-level difflib or `fuzz.ratio` at 0.45 is the **noise floor** for unrelated 1,500-character chunks, so the plan's "lexical ≥ 0.45" was meaningless. **Pin the lexical score:** word-level `SequenceMatcher(None, words_a, words_b, autojunk=False)`, symmetrised (mean of both directions), or 5-gram containment. All thresholds are starting values calibrated on the frozen gold, not sourced constants.
2. **`token_set_ratio` returns 100 for any token subset** ("…our business" vs "…our Chinese business"): never use it alone to call `reworded`. Use `fuzz.ratio` on headlines, and always pass `processor=rapidfuzz.utils.default_process` (rapidfuzz ≥ 3.0 no longer preprocesses; without it case and a trailing period alone moved 100 to 81). Pin `rapidfuzz>=3.14`.
3. **Text-grounded absence check (step 5):** needle = one sentence, haystack = the WHOLE section string (do not split the haystack); use `partial_ratio_alignment` so the verdict carries the matched span and a verbatim quote. Needles over 64 characters run in a non-exhaustive mode, so require **at least 2 of 3 probes** (headline + two longest sentences) to hit before `merged`; exactly 1 hit → `uncertain`. Same-company boilerplate (tax, insurance sentences, 88–99) can rescue a truly dropped risk: ignore needles in a boilerplate set. Measured: threshold 85 found 81–87% of true matches and 1.7% false ones (other company); only 65% two filings apart.
4. **`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` is a modified Jonker–Volgenant (Crouse 2016), not Hungarian, and always fully assigns the smaller side** (even at similarity 0.15): enforce the floor with dummy rows/columns valued at the floor. One-to-one cannot express merges; step 5 is the merge path.
5. **Unit detection:** SEC Item 105 (2020 amendment; Release 33-10825, adopted 2020-08-26, effective 2020-11-09) distinguishes per-risk *subcaptions* (our items) from group *headings* ("Risks Related to Our Industry", "General Risk Factors"), which must NOT become items. The rule does not cover Form 20-F (ASML, TSM): expect paragraph units there. The risk-factor summary block sits inside Item 1A for NVDA, AMD, MU, AVGO, META, QCOM; its bullets are near-verbatim headlines for AMD but paraphrases for NVDA and MU, so detect it by position and heading, not only by duplicate matching.
6. **No monotone/order constraint:** AVGO and ASML have ~30% out-of-order sentences. Vecalign (not on PyPI, needs a Cython build, forbids crossing alignments), SentAlign and Bertalign (GPL) are rejected; nothing maintained does item-level 10-K version alignment, so the layered approach stands.
7. **A possible data problem to check first:** ASML's prior-year sentences match the newer section at only 18% (others 42–96%) — probably a section-extraction problem; the unit-coverage gate must expose it.
8. **Citations fixed:** headings → SEC Release 33-10825; Brown, Tian, Tucker (2018, *Contemporary Accounting Research* 35(2)) is about spillover of SEC comment letters, measuring year-over-year Item 1A modification as 1 − cosine (after Brown & Tucker 2011, *JAR*), not headings; Cohen, Malloy, Nguyen (2020, *J. Finance* 75(3)) measured section/document-level change (cosine, Jaccard, edit distance, diff size) and is motivation, not validation of item-level detection.

## L. Corrections after running the real data (2026-09-26; these SUPERSEDE the matching details above)
Found by running item detection on the lake (W-A) and alignment on NVDA FY25→FY26 (free, before any gold was labelled).
1. **The "NAC / Hong-Kong items are Deleted" gate in H was based on a false premise.** NVIDIA discloses ONE export-control risk factor ("We are subject to complex laws, rules, regulations, and political and other actions…"), about 26k characters in both FY25 and FY26. It survives; what changed is sentences inside it. The measured facts: the NAC-process sentence ("the Notified Advanced Computing, or NAC, process has not resulted in approvals for exports of products to customers in China") and the sentence "we transitioned some operations … out of China and Hong Kong" are absent from the FY26 Item 1A; the AI Diffusion IFR paragraphs are gone; April-2025 H20 licensing text is new. **Item-level result on that pair (aligner, unlabelled): 7 unchanged, 16 reworded, 0 removed, 1 new ("Commercial arrangements expose us to counterparty risks"), 23 of 24 newer items carried.** The correct gate is therefore two-level: (a) item level: no false removed items on the flagship pair and the one genuinely new item is `new`; (b) **passage level**: the NAC sentence, the HK-transition sentence and the AI-Diffusion-IFR paragraph are reported as removed passages, and sentences whose only change is tense ("impact" → "impacted") are not.
2. **A change layer below the item is required.** `graph/alignment.py` already lists `Evidence.dropped_sentences` (sentences of a matched older item verifiably absent from the whole newer section). The M1b change layer is: for every matched (unchanged/reworded/merged) item pair, sentences grouped into contiguous **passages**, each classified `removed` (no near counterpart anywhere in the newer section), `reworded` (a near counterpart exists; both sides quoted) or, symmetrically from the newer side, `added`. Stored as `RiskPassage` nodes (`passage_id`, `kind`, `text`, `item_id`, `char_start`, `char_end`, `chunk_ids`), linked `(RiskItem)-[:HAS_PASSAGE]->(RiskPassage)`; the temporal block ranks passages under their item and every passage cites a chunk id of the filing it is quoted from. The answer wording is factual ("this sentence of the FY25 text no longer appears in the FY26 Item 1A"), never "the company dropped the risk" unless the whole item is removed. A sentence edited by ≥ 20% of its words can read as removed although a rewritten version exists: the `reworded` class and the gold-calibrated similarity cut exist for that, and passage precision is gated.
3. **Gold extends to sentences (before any labelling, so nothing is labelled twice).** For every non-removed older item in a seeded sample of items per development pair (always including the whole NVDA export-control item), labellers receive the item's mechanically split sentences and the full newer section and label each sentence `present` (verbatim/near quote, machine-checked), `reworded` (quote) or `removed` (≥ 3 search terms; rejected if the sentence is still there). Same three blind labellers, same agreement gate. Sampling is independent of the algorithm's output. Gates: **passage-level drop precision ≥ 0.90 and recall ≥ 0.80 on held-out pairs**, and the three named flagship passages must be hit.
4. **Pairs with an untrustworthy side are "not compared".** Coverage cannot vouch for paragraph units (they tile the section by construction), so a separate `section_suspect` flag (section length far from its neighbour's) exists, and both flags live in `data/interim/risk_items/<T>_risk_items_quality.json` (`parsing/risk_item_quality.py`). Alignment, the labelling tooling and the loader refuse such a pair; the graph stores `items_compared=false` and the reason on the `SUPERSEDES` edge; the temporal block says "not compared (reason)" rather than "nothing changed". Consequence on the current lake: **29 of 32 consecutive annual pairs are comparable; INTC (both older sides under 76% coverage: the section text runs ~29k characters past Item 1A) and the ASML FY24→FY25 pair (FY24 section text runs into sustainability chapters) are not**, and are excluded from held-out numbers. Fixing those section texts belongs to segmentation (a separate, later item); the loader must not paper over them.
5. **NVDA FY26 has 24 risk items, not the 28–30 estimated earlier.** The filing's own risk-factor summary lists 24 bullets and the item count matches it in all four NVDA years (25/24/23/24); AMD matches exactly (42/42). Nothing was loosened to reach 28.
6. **Contract answers for the loader** (from the parquet): `unit_kind` is exactly `headline` or `paragraph` (1,331 / 230 items); `headline` is never null (empty string for paragraph units); `chunk_ids` are in text order; `char_start`/`char_end` are section-text offsets and ARE stored on `RiskItem`; `lineage_id` is set by the loader for every item (a stable id carried along `SUCCEEDED_BY` chains); `decided_by` exists only on `SUCCEEDED_BY`, so removed/new lines omit it; a current annual has at most one predecessor with items.
7. **Graph contract for the change layer (definitive; the loader and the retriever are built to this).**
   - `(:RiskItem {item_id, accession_no, filer_cik, filing_date, section_id, seq, headline, text_hash, char_start, char_end, unit_kind ('headline'|'paragraph'), chunk_ids (text order), is_current, lineage_id, removed_in (accession of the newer filing that no longer contains it; null otherwise), is_new (true for a newer item with no older counterpart), snapshot_id})`.
   - `(older:RiskItem)-[:SUCCEEDED_BY {kind: 'unchanged'|'reworded'|'merged', decided_by, sim_embed, sim_lex}]->(newer:RiskItem)`.
   - `(:RiskPassage {passage_id, kind ('removed'|'reworded'|'added'), item_id, older_accession, newer_accession, filer_cik, text, counterpart_text (reworded only), similarity, char_start, char_end, chunk_ids (of the filing the text is quoted from), decided_by, snapshot_id})` with `(item:RiskItem {item_id})-[:HAS_PASSAGE]->(p)`; for `removed`/`reworded` the item is the OLDER item and the chunk ids are the older filing's; for `added` it is the NEWER item and the chunk ids are the newer filing's. Uniqueness on `passage_id`; index on `(filer_cik, newer_accession)`.
   - `(cur:Filing)-[:SUPERSEDES {kind: 'rolled', items_compared: boolean, not_compared_reason: string|null}]->(prev:Filing)`. When `items_compared` is false NO `SUCCEEDED_BY`, `removed_in`, `is_new` or `RiskPassage` exists for that pair, and every consumer must render "comparison not available (reason)", never "nothing changed".

   - **What was found.** The graph holds the text comparison of EVERY consecutive annual-filing pair (NVDA: FY2023->24, FY2024->25, FY2025->26), but `hybrid_retrieve` read only the pair whose newer filing is current, so a question about another pair was answered with the FY2025->26 comparison or with "no comparison available".    - **The rule** (`select_pairs`, `pair_selection_mode`, `ANNUAL_PAIRS_QUERY`, `TEMPORAL_SELECTED_QUERY`). NAMED: the question names fiscal years or period-end dates AND asks about a change in a disclosure (a risk-change word and a disclosure noun): the pairs whose two filings' fiscal years are both named, else whose newer filing's year is named, else whose older filing's year is; the `MAX_PAIRS_PER_COMPANY` newest. MULTI: the question asks about several annual reports ("across its recent annual reports", "earlier 10-Ks", "evolved"): the newest pairs the graph can read. DEFAULT: everything else reads the current pair through `TEMPORAL_QUERY`, unchanged. A filing's fiscal year is the year of the period end of its own annual XBRL metrics (a period of 300+ days on a `REPORTS_METRIC` edge whose `accession_no` is that filing), so "FY2025" is the 10-K for the fiscal year ending in 2025 (NVIDIA: January 2025). Pairs come back oldest first; the line caps of `TEMPORAL_CAPS` and `PASSAGE_CAPS` are split per pair when a company has several (each list keeps at least one line), and the totals are per pair, so "showing 4 of 6" never mixes two pairs. A named year with no pair in the graph gives the latest pair AND a notice ("no annual-filing comparison covering fiscal 2020 and 2021 is in the graph for Nvidia (annual filings loaded: ...); the latest comparison is shown instead"), never a silent substitute. A chosen pair with a side that has no risk items is not asked of the graph and renders "comparison not available (no risk items were loaded for the ... filed ...)"; a pair the loader marked `items_compared = false` passes through and renders the loader's own reason. Each chosen pair's section says which fiscal years it covers ("covers the fiscal year ended ... -> the fiscal year ended ..."; period-end wording on purpose, the answer prompt forbids "FY2025" labels).
   - **The guard.** Only a question about a risk-disclosure change WITH named years, or one asking about several annual reports, changes behaviour. A metric question that names years ("revenue in fiscal 2022"), a question with no year, and a risk question with no multi-year wording issue the same `TEMPORAL_QUERY` as before and render byte-identically (no `covers` line, no note); `tests/test_retrieval_pairs.py` pins the mode for every benchmark question (multi = exactly T1 and T3; named = temporal questions only, no numeric one). The real-Neo4j tests (`tests/integration/test_m1b_retrieval_neo4j.py`, classes `TestAnnualPairsQuery`, `TestNamedPairRetrieval`, `TestQuestionsThatNameNoPairKeepTheCurrentPairQuery`) run the queries, `hybrid_retrieve` and the block for every case above, and check that the FY2025->26 pair chosen by named years renders byte-identically to `TEMPORAL_SELECTED_QUERY` asked for that one accession alone and, but for its one `covers` line, to the default `TEMPORAL_QUERY` block (with two pairs of one company the caps are split, so a pair read inside a two-pair selection is not byte-identical to the same pair read alone).
   - **The limits.** (a) A filing whose XBRL is not loaded has no fiscal year and can never be selected by year (the notice lists only the years that are known). (b) `MAX_PAIRS_PER_COMPANY = 2`: a multi-year question reads the two newest pairs, and says when a third exists; NVDA's FY2023->24 pair is read only by a question that names its fiscal years. (c) Per-pair attribution of a removal claim is NOT checked: `removal_supported_ids` is the union of the removed lists of all sections, so a sentence about the FY2024->25 pair may cite an id that supports only a removal of the FY2025->26 pair; the check verifies "this id is under a removed heading", not "this id is under the removed heading of the pair the sentence is about". (d) FIXED the same day: when a named year has no pair and the current pair itself cannot be read (a side has no risk items), the notice used to say the latest comparison is shown although nothing was; it now says no readable comparison is available either (`test_a_notice_does_not_promise_a_latest_comparison_when_none_can_be_read`, unit and integration). (e) Loader follow-up: stamp `Filing.period_of_report` (or a fiscal-year property) at load time and `coalesce` it in `ANNUAL_PAIRS_QUERY`, so a filing's fiscal year no longer depends on its XBRL facts being present and first-disclosed under its own accession.
