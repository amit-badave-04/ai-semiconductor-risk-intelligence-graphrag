# Accepted risks

This is the record SDD stage 5 asks for: HIGH, MEDIUM and LOW findings that were declined or deferred, each with the owner's decision. A CRITICAL finding can never be accepted here; it blocks the merge until it is fixed. An entry records a decision about a known finding. It does not say the risk is gone, and each entry names the point at which it is looked at again.

IDs: L1 to L5 and I1 to I3 are the owner's labels for the LOW and INFO items of the round-3 reviews of the M5b I1 work (`docs/specs/m5b-frontend.md`, Status). They are not the plan's increments I1 to I5: the "I2" below is the second review item, not increment I2. By the owner's decision, review items L1, L2, L4 and I1 were fixed during increment I1; the four items below were deferred.

Two columns need care. "Owner decision" is the owner's call (2026-11-07, given in chat). "Why it is acceptable for now" is the reviewers' and the plan's rationale, recorded as they gave it; it is not the owner's words. In "Revisit at", the point for L3 is the owner's (moved to I4); the points for the other three are proposals that the owner has not set. On 2026-11-07 (round 5) the owner adopted the rationale of each of the four entries L3, L5, I2 and I3 as written; that adoption does not set the proposed revisit points. The entries R5-1 to R5-4 were recorded in round 5 and await the owner's decision.

| I3 | INFO | Three edge cases in the Lighthouse runner's stop, none of which holds the fixture port. (a) Chrome can outlive a SIGKILL escalation: `chrome-launcher` starts Chrome in its own session, so the kill sent to the Lighthouse process group never reaches it, and the only cleanup is the SIGTERM listener of `chrome-launcher`, which the escalation skips. (b) On POSIX the group SIGKILL is sent even after Node has reaped the group leader. That is safe while any member of the group is alive, and unsafe only if the whole group has exited, the port probe still reports open or unknown, and the pid is reused as a new group leader within the 10 s grace window. (c) On Windows, `taskkill /T` finds a process's children by their recorded parent pid, so a process whose own parent died, with that dead parent's pid now held by the server, could be counted in the server's tree; the stop-race review's W2 guard protects only the root pid (not verified). The reviewers' optional fixes: have `lighthouse.mjs` launch Chrome itself so the runner owns its pid; on POSIX skip the group SIGKILL when the leader has exited and the group is gone; on Windows document the limit. | `web/scripts/fixture_guard.mjs` (`signalTree`, `stopServerProcess`), `web/scripts/lighthouse.mjs` (the Lighthouse child that starts Chrome) | Round-3 Opus security review (INFO, twice); round-3 code review (INFO) | 2026-11-07, deferred: recorded, not fixed in I1; owner adopts the rationale above, 2026-11-07 | Judged very unlikely.
