  Live questions cost real money (one or two model calls each), so they are rate-limited per address and capped per day; benchmark questions are served from a saved evaluation run. Financial figures come from XBRL facts, not from model parsing. Each answer is checked so that every cited id was retrieved and every number matches the retrieved context; those checks do not prove that a sentence is supported by the passage it cites, so read the source. Source code and methodology: GitHub.
  
    
  
    How reliable are change claims? Statements about what changed between two annual filings come from an automatic text comparison, and each kind is worded only as strongly as it held up when two people checked a small held-out sample by hand (the samples are small, so read the counts as rough guides). A risk factor labelled “no longer appears as a separate risk factor”: 4 of 4 checked cases were gone as a standalone risk factor, but 2 of the 4 had been folded into another risk factor rather than dropped, so it does not mean the company stopped disclosing the risk. “Not matched”: the text check could not settle these older risk factors, so they are never shown as removed. “No matching risk factor found in the earlier filing”: only 6 of 12 checked cases were really new; the other 6 were already disclosed earlier, for example split out of an older risk factor. A sentence whose “wording was not found” in the other filing: 6 of the 51 sentences flagged as missing from the newer filing were still stated in different words (sentences flagged as new did better: 1 of 48).
